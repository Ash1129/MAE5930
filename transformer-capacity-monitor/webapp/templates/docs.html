What the monitor measures

The monitor watches every distribution transformer in Tompkins County against its nameplate rating. Each run takes the {{ playbook.period_days }}-day review week, draws the daily peak load on a control chart built from the {{ playbook.history_days }} days before it, measures the room left below the rating, projects heat-pump growth, and sorts the transformer into one of three tiers: within limits, review needed or inspect now.

Every statistic and every tier is a fixed rule written down in the playbook and the decision table. A named reviewer records a decision on each flagged transformer before the week's report can be finalized. The tool never changes equipment or operating settings.

Rules & tiers

Runs happen on the playbook schedule ({{ playbook.schedule.cron }}, {{ playbook.timezone }}): Monday, Wednesday and Friday at 06:00. Each one scores the {{ playbook.period_days }} days ending the day before.

The control chart

One value per transformer per day, its daily peak load, on an individuals and moving-range (I-MR) chart. The centre line and limits come from the transformer's own {{ playbook.history_days }}-day baseline, never from the rating: centre = mean, UCL/LCL = mean ± {{ playbook.chart.E2 }} × MR-bar, MR UCL = {{ playbook.chart.D4 }} × MR-bar. Four run rules apply: a point beyond 3 sigma, 2 of 3 beyond 2 sigma on one side, 8 in a row on one side, and a moving range above its UCL. A chart needs at least {{ playbook.chart.min_history_points }} baseline days.

Room left today

For a stable chart, a one-sided capability index against the rating, Cpk = (rating − mean) ÷ 3σ with σ = MR-bar ÷ {{ playbook.chart.d2 }}, and a 95% interval (Bissell's approximation, effective degrees of freedom {{ playbook.capability.mr_effective_df_factor }} × (n − 1)). It is withheld when the daily peaks fail a lag-1 autocorrelation or normality check. Review below {{ playbook.capability.cpk_review_below }}.

The winter estimate

This week's peak plus customers × (forecast heat-pump share − today's share) × kW per heat pump ÷ {{ playbook.forecast.power_factor }} power factor, using the forecast for the transformer's declared area. Review when it reaches {{ (playbook.forecast.near_rating_ratio * 100)|round|int }}% of the rating. It is always labelled as an estimate.

First matching row wins. Rules 1 to 3 are inspect now, 4 to 12 are review needed, and 13 is within limits. Thresholds are read from rules/playbook.json (version {{ playbook.version }}) when the table loads, so there is one place to change them.

A run refuses incomplete or mismatched inputs and posts an alert instead of publishing a partial report: a missing file, a transformer without a rating, a boundary file valid for another period, or days without readings all stop it before analysis.

Everything in this copy is synthetic. The 500 transformers, their readings, the boundaries' assignment of transformers, the forecast and the outside-air feed are generated by scripts/make_synthetic_data.py with a fixed seed, and planted cases exercise each rule. Replace the feeds with exports from your meter, asset, GIS and forecast systems, keep the same columns, and run a period. The codebook describes every column and how to collect it for real.

Setup

Two settings live in the app; everything else is a file or an environment variable, so nothing secret is ever typed here.

Reviewer

The named grid-planning reviewer receives the review queue and is the only one who can finalize a week's report.

AI explanations optional, off by default

When on, the map popup adds a one or two sentence summary of each transformer and each new alert is reworded in plain English, both written by Claude Haiku (claude-haiku-5-5) through the claude command-line tool on this computer, on your own Claude subscription. The rule-based text always stays as the fallback; Claude never changes a result, threshold or setting, and the alert text is passed to it as data so wording inside a data file cannot steer it. Each distinct alert is explained once and cached.

FAQ

{{ chev }}Why did a transformer turn amber or red?
Open it from the map or the dashboard: the top of its page names the rule that matched and says what happened in plain words. Red means a reading reached the rating, a run rule fired this week, or three or more days fell outside the normal range. Amber means a few outliers, thin or uncertain headroom, a winter estimate near the rating, or not enough history yet.
{{ chev }}What does “modeled” mean?
Anything with that badge is calculated from the recorded loads and published formulas, not measured: the seasonal heat view, the winding and oil temperatures in the cutaway, and the winter estimate. The tool has no sensors inside a transformer and no weather station of its own. Modeled values are for understanding; no rule reads them.
{{ chev }}Does the tool change any equipment or settings?
No. It reads files, draws charts, and records the reviewer's decisions. Nothing it does reaches a transformer, a switch or a SCADA system.
{{ chev }}Where is AI used?
Nowhere in a statistic or a decision. One optional setting, off by default, asks the local claude command-line tool to reword alert and summary text in plain language; the rule-based text is always kept and nothing it writes changes a result.
{{ chev }}Why is a control limit not the rating?
Control limits describe how much a transformer's own load usually varies; the rating is the nameplate. A transformer can be well inside its limits and still close to its rating, or far below its rating and still show a real change in pattern. The pages show both.
{{ chev }}Can I run it on real data?
Yes. Keep the column names in the codebook, drop the exports into data/feeds/, approve the thresholds in rules/playbook.json, and run a period. Replace the synthetic boundaries and forecast with the county GIS layer and the planning team's forecast first.
