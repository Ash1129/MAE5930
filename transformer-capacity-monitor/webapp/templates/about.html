About

Who we are

A Cornell team building a fixed-rules monitor for the distribution transformers that keep Tompkins County's lights on.

Why we do this

The Transformer Capacity Monitor watches every distribution transformer in the county against its nameplate rating, three times a week, and tells a grid-planning engineer which few need a look: within limits, review needed, or inspect now.

Distribution transformers are the last step between the grid and a street, and they are sized for the load a street used to have. Heat pumps change that: a neighbourhood that electrifies its heating can push a transformer past its rating on a cold evening years before anyone planned to replace it. Utilities have the hourly readings to see this coming, but five hundred transformers produce more charts than a person can read every week.

So we built a monitor that reads them all with the same fixed rules, statistical process control on each transformer's own history plus a capability check against its rating, and shows the engineer only the exceptions, with the reason in plain words and a record of the decision taken. Every statistic and tier is a written rule; nothing is left to a model's judgement.
